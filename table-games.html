Blackjack: rule variations and the basic-strategy floor

Blackjack is the most rule-sensitive game in the casino. The same surface game runs from 0.4% house edge (single deck, dealer stands soft 17, double after split, late surrender, resplit aces) to over 1.5% (eight decks, dealer hits soft 17, no double after split, no surrender, blackjack pays 6:5 instead of 3:2). The rule changes that move the math most:

  • Blackjack pays 3:2 vs 6:5. The single most damaging rule change. 6:5 adds ~1.4% to house edge. Avoid 6:5 tables unless rules elsewhere compensate.
  • Dealer stands on soft 17 (S17) vs hits soft 17 (H17). H17 adds ~0.2% to house edge.
  • Number of decks. Single-deck rules-equal: ~0.17% edge. Six-deck: ~0.6%. Eight-deck: ~0.65%.
  • Double after split. Allowed: -0.14% edge for the player.
  • Surrender (late). Allowed: -0.07% edge for the player.

Basic strategy — a 4×13 decision matrix for every player hand against every dealer upcard — is legal in every regulated jurisdiction and reduces house edge to its theoretical minimum for the rule set. Memorize it for one rule set first (six-deck S17), then expand. Card counting is mathematically irrelevant against an RNG — the deck is reshuffled every hand.

Roulette: the layout matters more than the strategy

European roulette (37-pocket, single zero) carries 2.7% house edge on every bet. American roulette (38-pocket, single + double zero) carries 5.26% on most bets and 7.89% on the five-number basket bet. French roulette adds La Partage or En Prison rules to even-money outside bets, halving their effective edge to 1.35%. The 888bet RNG roulette lobby leads with European, with French where licensed. Avoid American unless you have a specific reason — the math is roughly twice as expensive.

Inside vs outside bets: outside bets (red/black, even/odd, columns, dozens) carry the same 2.7% edge but lower variance. Inside bets (single number, splits, corners, streets) carry the same edge but higher variance. Betting strategy doesn’t change house edge — Martingale, D’Alembert, Fibonacci all eventually meet table maximums and lose to variance. Bet for entertainment within a fixed budget.

Baccarat: banker, player, never tie

Baccarat’s three-way bet structure has the second-lowest house edge available on a casino floor. Banker bet 1.06% (after 5% commission), player bet 1.24%, tie bet 14.36%. The math is decided by fixed third-card draw rules; there is no skill component on the main bets. Side bets — Banker Pair, Player Pair, Either Pair, Perfect Pair, Dragon Bonus — carry 5–11% house edge. Stick to banker for the lowest expected loss; treat side bets as occasional entertainment within your stake budget.

Video poker & house-banked poker variants

Video poker is unusual: with optimal play on a full-pay paytable, several variants beat 99% RTP — Jacks or Better full-pay (9/6) returns 99.54%, Deuces Wild full-pay returns 100.76% (theoretically positive expected value, but operators rarely offer the full paytable). Watch for short paytables (8/5 or 7/5 Jacks or Better) which knock 1–2% off RTP without changing the visual. Three Card Poker base game: 3.37% house edge on Ante/Play, Pair Plus side bet 7.28%. Caribbean Stud: 5.22% house edge with optimal play, plus a separate progressive jackpot side bet that’s mathematically negative for most stakes but offers the lottery-style upside.
