Introduction to World Cup Host Cities 2026

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is set to be an unprecedented event, uniting fans around the globe in a celebration of football. For the first time in history, the tournament will be hosted by three nations: the United States, Canada, and Mexico. This collaborative effort aims to enhance the World Cup experience by featuring a wider array of venues and cultures. With 16 host cities lined up, fans can expect a diverse array of stadiums, local flavors, and vibrant atmospheres. Whether you're a long-time football enthusiast or new to the sport, this guide delves into the host cities and their iconic stadiums, providing essential information for every fan planning to attend.

Each of the selected cities offers a unique perspective on American life, blending sports with the local culture. Cities have been carefully chosen to reflect a combination of legacy, culture, and infrastructure that can handle the influx of fans. For football supporters, this tournament promises not only thrilling matches but also a rich journey through some of North America’s most exciting locations. From lively urban centers to vibrant local traditions, the host cities for World Cup 2026 will have much to offer.

Atlanta: Mercedes-Benz Stadium

Located at the heart of Atlanta, the Mercedes-Benz Stadium is known for its innovative design and state-of-the-art facilities. This stadium is not only home to the NFL's Atlanta Falcons but also features a retractable roof that opens up to the scenic skyline of the city. The stadium's architecture and cutting-edge technology make it a phenomenal venue for hosting high-profile sporting events, which is why it's well-suited for the World Cup. With a capacity of around 71,000, fans can expect an electric atmosphere as they cheer for their teams.

One of the standout features of Mercedes-Benz Stadium is its commitment to sustainability. It has earned LEED Platinum certification, reflecting its environmentally friendly initiatives and energy-efficient design. Additional features include a large video board that enhances the viewing experience and a spacious concourse lined with unique food options, emphasizing Atlanta's vibrant culinary scene.

Fans visiting Atlanta have plenty to explore beyond the stadium. The city is rich in history and culture, hosting attractions such as the Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park and the Georgia Aquarium, one of the largest in the world. This combination of sports and culture allows visitors to appreciate the city’s heartbeat while enjoying world-class football.

Boston: Gillette Stadium

Situated in Foxborough, just a short drive from downtown Boston, Gillette Stadium is an iconic venue that epitomizes New England's passion for sports. As home to the New England Patriots and the Revolution, this stadium encompasses a vibrant sporting culture. With a seating capacity of approximately 65,000, Gillette Stadium is designed to provide unparalleled sightlines for spectators. The open-air design offers fans an incredible view of the football field while being immersed in the passionate atmosphere characteristic of New England football.

In addition to hosting football games, Gillette Stadium is a multi-purpose venue where concerts and other significant events take place. Its state-of-the-art sound and lighting systems make it a sought-after location for many world-famous performers, contributing to the region’s entertainment landscape.

For fans visiting during the World Cup, the experience extends beyond live matches. Boston's rich history, from the Freedom Trail to its historic landmarks, provides a rich background for visitors. The city is renowned for its academic institutions, museums, and a thriving arts scene. Enjoying local delicacies such as clam chowder or lobster rolls will enrich your Boston experience. Gillette Stadium and the surrounding attractions offer an ideal environment for fans to dive into sports and culture.

Dallas: AT&T Stadium

Known as 'Jerry World,' AT&T Stadium is a marvel of modern sports engineering located in Arlington, Texas. With space for approximately 100,000 fans and the ability to increase capacity for special events, the stadium boasts one of the largest HD video screens in the world, allowing fans to catch every goal and play from any angle. As home to the Dallas Cowboys, this structure represents a benchmark in sports architecture and atmosphere. The World Cup event promises to be nothing short of electrifying in this venue.

One of the unique aspects of AT&T Stadium is its innovative virtual reality experiences and immersive fan engagement technologies. This adds a new layer of interaction for fans attending matches, allowing them to enjoy various entertainment options throughout their visit.

Dallas is filled with cultural experiences that complement the excitement of the World Cup. From the vibrant art scene in the Arts District to the culinary delights in the bustling food markets and restaurants, visitors can indulge in a variety of cultural offerings while preparing for matches. Additionally, historical sites like Dealey Plaza and the Sixth Floor Museum offer insights into the rich tapestry of American history, making Dallas a city where sports and culture thrive together.

Los Angeles: SoFi Stadium

SoFi Stadium, the latest addition to the NFL family, encapsulates the glamour and excitement of Los Angeles. Located in Inglewood, this stadium has a seating capacity of approximately 70,000 and features a unique design that integrates both indoor and outdoor environments. Highlighted by its cutting-edge features and surrounding facilities, SoFi Stadium promises an unforgettable atmosphere during the World Cup. As the home of the Los Angeles Rams and Chargers, its design reflects the high standards of the entertainment capital of the world.

The stadium's amenities prioritize fan comfort and engagement, offering various dining options and fan experiences within its walls. Moreover, its proximity to Los Angeles means that fans can explore a vast array of attractions, from Hollywood landmarks to the stunning beaches of Santa Monica. Those attending matches can easily enjoy a sunny day out at the beach or perhaps visit famous museums, creating a well-rounded experience.

Los Angeles also boasts a diverse culinary landscape, from food trucks to world-renowned fine dining. The vibrant arts scene, spearheaded by institutions like the Getty Center and various art galleries, enhances the cultural richness surrounding the stadium, making it an invaluable part of the World Cup 2026 experience.

Miami: Hard Rock Stadium

Located in the lively city of Miami, the Hard Rock Stadium is a true gem in the world of sports venues. With a capacity that exceeds 65,000, it serves as home to the Miami Dolphins and regularly hosts major events like the Super Bowl. Its tropical surroundings are matched by the stadium’s vibrant atmosphere and incredible acoustics, making it a wonderful venue for football fans. During the World Cup, attendees can look forward to matches infused with the colorful local culture, all while basking in the sunny weather Miami is known for.

The stadium features several fan-friendly initiatives designed to maximize enjoyment. With numerous food options reflecting Miami's multicultural flair, fans can indulge in everything from Cuban sandwiches to fresh seafood. The Hard Rock Stadium also incorporates state-of-the-art technology, ensuring that every fan has a great view and experience throughout the event.

The allure of Miami extends beyond the stadium. Its famous beaches, lively nightlife, and cultural festivals create a dynamic environment for World Cup attendees. Explore the Art Deco District, enjoy the food scene in Little Havana, or dance the night away in South Beach. All these facets make Miami a destination not just for football, but for an all-encompassing cultural experience.

New York: MetLife Stadium

Just outside of New York City, MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, stands as a key venue for the World Cup 2026. With a seating capacity of around 82,500, it is the largest stadium in the New York metropolitan area. This venue regularly hosts major NFL games and large events, making it one of the most significant sports facilities in the country. The allure of New York City adds to the excitement – fans can explore iconic attractions like Times Square and Central Park, ensuring a truly unforgettable World Cup experience.

MetLife Stadium's architectural design incorporates a blend of modern facilities with legacy charm, providing fans both comfort and excitement. Its proximity to NYC means easy access to world-class dining, shopping, and entertainment options. Visitors can enjoy everything from Broadway plays to global cuisine from every corner of the world.

As a part of the World Cup festivities, visitors to New York can partake in various events occurring around the city. The vibrant atmosphere, infused with the excitement of international football, is complemented by street celebrations, fan zones, and cultural exhibits. New York truly offers an unforgettable experience that combines sports with its diverse city life.

Philadelphia: Lincoln Financial Field

Lincoln Financial Field, located in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, is known for its passionate fan base and rich sports history. With a capacity of around 69,000, this stadium is the home of the Philadelphia Eagles and ensures a roaring atmosphere for every match. It provides a range of amenities designed to enhance the spectator experience, including a variety of food and drink options, comfortable seating, and modern facilities, ensuring that fans can enjoy the excitement of the World Cup in comfort.

Beyond the walls of the stadium, Philadelphia is steeped in history. The city offers numerous iconic sights, from the Liberty Bell to the Philadelphia Museum of Art, known for its famous “Rocky Steps.” Fans attending matches can also indulge in Philly's famous cheesesteaks and explore local breweries, enhancing their experience of both sports and culture.

Philadelphia's blend of sports legacy, historical significance, and vibrant atmosphere makes it an excellent host for the World Cup. The spirit of the city thrives during sporting events, allowing fans to feel a strong sense of community and passion for the game.
