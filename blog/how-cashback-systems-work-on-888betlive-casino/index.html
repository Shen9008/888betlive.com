What cashback is (and is not)

Net-loss arithmetic

Industry-standard formulas start with net gaming revenue on eligible titles during the window:

  • Baseline: total stakes − total returns (wins + refund voids).
  • Percentage: published rate × net loss, often tiered (e.g., 5% below 2,000 units loss, 8% above).
  • Caps: maximum cashback per player per cycle—watch for split caps across product verticals.

Worked example without currency: stakes 1,000, wins 910 ⇒ net loss 90; 10% scheme ⇒ 9 units before tax/fees. If the promo excludes bonus-wallet turnover, only cash-wallet bets count—mixing wallets zeroes the numerator silently.

Why operators publish it

From the house perspective, cashback lowers churn on losing weeks—players who receive an 8-unit rebate after a 100-unit downswing exhibit measurably higher 30-day retention in cohort studies (internal operator analytics commonly cite mid-single-digit lifts). From your perspective, it trims downside volatility slightly while preserving responsible messaging: rebates should never justify chasing losses.

  • When will I receive my cashback? Settlement can be hourly, daily, or weekly. Credits may arrive instantly after settlement or within 24–72 hours for fraud review—always read the promo clock on the offers page.

  • Do all games contribute? Rarely equally. Slots often count 100% of turnover toward cashback formulas while live blackjack may count 10–20%. Some jackpots or promo titles can be excluded entirely.
